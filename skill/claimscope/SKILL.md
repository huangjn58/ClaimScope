# ClaimScope

**Strong claims. Exact boundaries. Evidence first.**

Write the strongest claim your evidence can actually support. Coordinate claim,
evidence, scope and narrative; do not simply make every sentence sound stronger.
Preserve the user's language, terminology, discipline and requested depth of revision.

## Decision priority

1. Scientific truth and evidence boundaries.
2. User-provided facts and explicit constraints.
3. Claim–evidence consistency.
4. Press-Release Principle.
5. Anti-defensive writing.
6. Section-specific academic logic.
7. Concision.
8. Stylistic optimization.

Conflicting source facts require clarification, not silent selection. A user request
to strengthen prose does not authorize altering evidence or removing decisive limits.

## Default workflow

1. Identify the section's argumentative duty and the requested mode: conservative
   revision, deep restructuring, drafting, or audit without rewriting.
2. Identify the central claim and map it to supplied observations, analyses or sources.
   Separate observations, interpretations and untested hypotheses.
3. Check whether the evidence supports the claim's strength and scope. Preserve
   numerical values, equations, citations and scientific meaning; flag contradictions.
4. Inspect defensive language by function, not by a prohibited-word list. Read the
   boundaries module when a qualification may affect accuracy.
5. Remove empty disclaimers; retain necessary conditions as positive scope and precise
   qualification. Narrow unsupported claims instead of adding vague hedging.
6. Organize the strongest supported contribution: important problem → actual gap →
   contribution → evidence → significance. Preserve decisive counterevidence.
7. Apply section-specific logic and remove formulaic transitions, redundant contrasts
   and inflated prose. Do not replace clear short sentences with vague long ones.
8. Recheck facts, causal strength, uncertainty, scope, and any proposed new work.
   Deliver usable text by default; explain edits when requested. An audit request gets
   findings, not an unsolicited rewrite. Briefly disclose material unresolved issues.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Never invent data, citations, methods, significance, completed revisions or locations.
- Never turn association into causation, calibration into independent validation,
  nonsignificance into equivalence, or local evidence into universal proof.
- Do not hide key negative results, change prespecified outcomes covertly, or remove
  limitations needed for validity, transparency or correct use of a result.
- Do not invent weaknesses or attack the author on behalf of an imagined reviewer.
  Necessary uncertainty is not a stylistic defect. Do not promise AI-detector evasion.
- Do not expand experiments, simulations, research objects or validation requirements
  without explicit authorization. Identify a genuine core evidence gap when present;
  first consider supported wording, evidence organization and a narrower claim.
- A reviewer request is not authorization to undertake or promise new research.
- Follow applicable user and project instructions. When combining writing skills,
  use this skill for claim–evidence–scope decisions and specialized tools for formatting;
  style cannot override accuracy. Do not edit other skills or configuration.
