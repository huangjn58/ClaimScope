# Doctoral Thesis Style

**What should this section actually do?**

Preserve discipline-specific terminology and the manuscript's language. English
instructions do not require English output. Give each section a scientific job.

## Abstract

State the problem, method, key quantitative findings, contribution and significance.
Use supplied numbers, not illustrative ones. Avoid generic limitation paragraphs;
retain any condition essential to understanding the headline claim correctly.

## Introduction

Move from relevant background to the scientific question, existing research,
unresolved gap, and this work's objectives and contributions. Do not front-load
imagined reviewer objections or turn the introduction into an activity log.

## Literature Review

Organize around questions, mechanisms or methodological disagreements rather than
author-by-author summaries. Distinguish evidence, mechanism, methodology and scale
gaps. Establish them from verified sources; do not diminish prior work to create novelty.
Never invent references or claim an exhaustive search without performing it.

## Methods

Prioritize reproducibility: apparatus, calibration, parameter and variable definitions,
units, specimen or sample preparation, experimental path, boundary conditions,
replication and data processing. State real design limits without apology. Mark missing
details as requiring confirmation instead of supplying plausible fictional methods.

## Results

Lead with an observed finding and its quantitative support, comparison, characteristic
values and available statistical evidence. Keep numbers consistent with tables and
figures. Include real variability and negative findings that affect the claim.
Do not silently promote trends to significance or observations to causal mechanisms.
In combined Results and Discussion sections, distinguish these argumentative roles.

## Discussion

Explain mechanisms, interpretation, comparison, implications, applicability and
scientific boundaries. Separate measured evidence from mechanistic inference.
For example, an observed deformation curve alone does not establish particle breakage
or a microscopic structural mechanism. Consolidate actual limitations here when
appropriate; keep uncertainty with the exact claim when needed.

## Conclusions

Answer the scientific question with the most important supported findings. Retain
necessary scope and quantitative results. Introduce neither new findings nor generic
self-criticism. Do not end with an unrequested list of future experiments. A required
outlook must remain distinct from completed work.

## Contributions and whole-thesis structure

Distinguish a new finding, new method and a new application of an existing method.
Workload is not novelty. Do not assert priority or superiority without comparative
evidence. Map chapter questions to the thesis question; keep symbols and terminology
consistent without forcing every chapter to prove the same claim.

## Proposals, reports and presentations

Distinguish completed evidence, proposed tasks and expected outcomes. Do not conceal
unfinished deliverables in a project report. Organize presentations around the question,
contribution and decisive figures; shorter slides still require honest evidence boundaries.

## Style

Use calm, specific, professional prose. Remove empty praise, repeated transitions,
mechanical triads and needless “not X but Y” framing. Retain useful conceptual contrasts.
Prefer precise short sentences over inflated complexity. Do not add personal anecdotes,
fake authorial experience or detector-evasion language.
