# Press-Release Principle

**What deserves the spotlight?**

Inspired by [Adkid-Zephyr's contribution-centered writing framework](https://github.com/Adkid-Zephyr/anti-defensive-writing-Skill).
Treat a paper as a scientific announcement, not a project diary or a self-audit.
This is a narrative principle, not permission to market unsupported conclusions.

## Build the argument

Establish why the problem matters, what verified gap remains, what this work offers,
which evidence supports it, and why that finding matters. Select the strongest
supported contribution, not the most impressive-sounding one.

Give each experiment or analysis an argumentative duty: support a claim, assess a
mechanism, establish usefulness under a stated constraint, or evaluate a relevant
alternative explanation. These are roles for existing evidence, not a checklist that
automatically requires new experiments.

Do not give every result equal narrative weight. Essential evidence belongs in the
main argument; auxiliary material may be summarized or placed in an appropriate
appendix. Preserve reporting obligations, primary outcomes and decisive counterevidence.
Chronology belongs in methods when sequence matters; effort and activity are not contributions.

## Handle an unfavorable comparison

1. Determine whether it bears on the central claim, validity or correct application.
2. If not central, it need not define the paper's main contribution. Preserve records
   and required reporting; do not erase inconvenient data.
3. If relevant, report the result accurately and explain its physical or practical meaning.
4. Discuss different objectives, constraints or trade-offs only when evidence supports them.
5. Narrow the claim when necessary. A local disadvantage does not establish general failure.

Do not make an irrelevant metric the defining competition. Conversely, never hide a
prespecified primary metric, retroactively change the objective without disclosure,
or disguise a failed central test as an unrelated detail. A changed comparison needs
an explicit rationale; evidence consistency outranks narrative convenience.

## Detect unnecessary self-weakening

Inspect phrases such as “unfortunately,” “only,” “limited effectiveness,” “we do not
claim,” “much work remains,” and “results should be interpreted cautiously.” Chinese
signals include “遗憾的是”“必须承认”“仅”“只是”“仍明显落后”“效果有限”“尚不能”
“由于条件限制”“不能排除”“本文并不试图”“虽然……但是仍然”“仍需大量研究”。

These are inspection signals, not banned words. “Only two specimens” can state an
essential sample size. Use the [functional classification](scientific-boundaries.md)
before deleting information. Do not apologize for questions the author never posed,
invent limitations, or promote a weak local metric into a global methodological flaw.

## Final narrative check

- Can the reader identify the central claim and its evidence?
- Does each paragraph have an argumentative job?
- Are adverse findings reported where they change interpretation?
- Are necessary limits precise rather than scattered apologies?
- Did restructuring preserve the original scientific facts and stated study objectives?

Do not manufacture novelty, superiority or mechanisms. Scientific truth and
claim–evidence consistency always outrank the Press-Release Principle.
