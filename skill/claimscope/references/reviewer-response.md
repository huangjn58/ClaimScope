# Reviewer Response

**Does this reviewer comment really require another experiment?**

Handle journal reviews, rebuttals, thesis examiner comments and revision statements.
Understand the concern before drafting a response; do not treat criticism as an attack.

## Evidence-first response sequence

Clarify wording → reorganize existing evidence → define scope → strengthen the
supported mechanism explanation → adjust the claim.

Use the steps that address the actual concern. Existing analyses may be presented
more clearly; proposed or unperformed analyses must not be described as completed.
Figure captions, variable definitions and terminology can resolve ambiguity without
expanding the study. Real errors require correction, not rhetorical reframing.

## Keep research commitments explicit

Do not automatically add experiments, simulations, objects, conditions or validation
metrics. A reviewer's suggestion is not the author's authorization to undertake work.
If the central claim genuinely cannot be sustained, identify the precise evidence gap
and first consider a defensible narrower claim. Do not hide that gap behind “out of scope.”
Ask for an author decision before proposing or committing to expanded research.

## Response structure

Acknowledge the issue briefly, state the response, explain the evidence or reason,
and identify the actual revision location. Avoid generic “revised accordingly” replies.
If a comment is not adopted, explain why in terms of the research question and evidence,
without dismissing the reviewer or promising unsupported superiority.

Only write “we have revised” when the revision is confirmed. A draft response should
use accurate prospective wording and identify what remains to be done. Do not invent
page, line or figure numbers; request or flag missing locations outside the final letter.

## Tone and delivery

Be professional, confident, evidence-based and non-confrontational. Apologize briefly
for an actual error when appropriate; do not reflexively write “We sincerely apologize”
for every request for clarification. Do not strengthen a causal interpretation solely
because the reviewer asks for a mechanism.

Return ready-to-use point-by-point text unless the user requests an audit. Distinguish
completed revisions from proposed ones. For validity or uncertainty issues, also read
[Scientific Boundaries](scientific-boundaries.md).
