# Scientific Boundaries

**How strong can this claim legitimately be?**

Inspired by [Kiterlin's distinction between defensive prose and necessary limits](https://github.com/Kiterlin/anti-defensive-writing).
Before removing a qualification, ask whether it affects validity, interpretation,
application, research design, methodological transparency or correct use of the result.
If so, preserve the information, even in an abstract or conclusion.

## Classify the function

| Class | Function | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. unnecessary disclaimer | Defends against an imagined criticism without information | Delete |
| B. necessary scope condition | Defines studied materials, settings, time or parameters | Rewrite as positive scope |
| C. real methodological limitation | Changes what the design or measurement can establish | Retain precisely and place appropriately |
| D. evidence-based qualification | Expresses genuine uncertainty | Retain and identify its supported source |
| E. useful conceptual contrast | Distinguishes concepts necessary to the argument | Retain |
| F. redundant clarification | Repeats information already unambiguous | Delete or merge |

A sentence can contain several classes. Split it rather than making a wholesale
deletion. When context is insufficient, preserve the qualification and flag the
missing evidence; do not guess that it is unnecessary.

## Positive scope + precise qualification

Replace a defensive denial of universal applicability with the actual investigated
conditions. Name the parameter range when supplied; do not invent it.

Replace vague stacked hedges with a specific observation when evidence is sufficient.
When uncertainty is real, identify its known source: sampling, measurement error,
confounding, model assumptions or incomplete identification. Do not fabricate an
uncertainty source merely to make a sentence sound precise.

“Suggests” and “may be associated with” are appropriate for an unverified mechanism.
“Demonstrates,” “proves” and “is caused by” cannot replace them without stronger evidence.
A descriptive trend is not statistical significance; agreement with a curve is not
independent validation or proof of the underlying mechanism.

## Placement

Document design and measurement limits in methods; report uncertainty with results;
discuss implications for mechanism and extrapolation in discussion. Consolidate
repeated disclaimers, but repeat a short essential condition in independently read
abstracts, captions or conclusions when its absence would mislead.

Actual flaws must not be relabeled as harmless scope. For example, sensor saturation
that prevents peak measurement is an inference limitation, not merely a choice of range.

## Protect the evidence

Keep decisive negative results, relevant alternative explanations and actual statistical
uncertainty. Do not hide contradictory observations, infer equivalence from a
nonsignificant test, conflate association and causation, or generalize beyond the design.
Necessary limits on safety, ethics or lawful use must also remain.

When a broad claim fails, state the strongest narrower claim the existing evidence
supports. If none is supported, state that evidence gap; do not invent a substitute result.
